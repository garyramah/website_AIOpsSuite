What is AIOps?

AIOps — artificial intelligence for IT operations, a term Gartner defined in 2016 — applies machine learning and big-data analytics to the enormous volumes of data IT environments already emit. It is a shift from isolated data in separate tools to one aggregated picture: observational data (logs, metrics, traces) joined with interaction data (events, tickets, incidents), analyzed continuously, with automation on top.

Why a suite — and why now

Modern estates are hybrid and multi-cloud, stitched from microservices and APIs whose dependencies change faster than anyone can document. That complexity is exactly what AIOps was defined to handle. Industry analyses cited on Wikipedia report mean-time-to-detect improvements of 15–20% and critical-incident reductions of over 50% from AI-driven end-to-end service management — the prize for getting this right is measured in outages that never reach customers.

Questions, answered straight

Does it replace our monitoring tools?

No. It sits on top of them. Your existing tools keep collecting; the suite aggregates, correlates, and acts on what they emit. Rip-and-replace is how AIOps projects die.

Does it fix things on its own?

Routine fixes, yes — within runbooks you approve. Consequential changes wait for a human. A person oversees and validates the model’s conclusions, and the system keeps the record either way.

How is this different from DevOps or MLOps?

DevOps accelerates software delivery; MLOps manages the lifecycle of machine-learning models. AIOps operates the systems themselves — detecting, diagnosing, and resolving what happens in production. They complement each other; none replaces the others.

What data does it need?

What you already have: system logs and metrics, network data, real-time operations events, historical performance data, and incident and ticketing records. The suite’s first job is making that data work together.
